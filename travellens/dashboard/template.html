How to read this. Bar length shows how much visitors discussed an aspect; the red share shows how much of that discussion was negative. Aspects with fewer than 5 opinions are not shown at all — three complaints is an anecdote, not a finding. Aspects with 5–14 opinions carry a low flag.

Important limitation. Complaint rate is the share of expressed opinions that are negative — not the share of visitors who had a problem. People rarely write reviews praising a road that simply worked, so every aspect is biased toward complaint once mentioned. Read these rates comparatively between destinations, never as an absolute risk.

Two corpora, two audiences. 30,705 Google Maps reviews (collected March 2024; 236 destinations; predominantly local reviewers; no star ratings; relative timestamps only) and 16,149 TripAdvisor reviews (2011–2023; 76 locations; overwhelmingly international reviewers — UK, Australia and India account for most of them; star-rated; absolute dates). 46,854 reviews in total. The two populations are kept separable, because what local and international visitors complain about is a question this data can answer rather than something to average away.

Coverage. 19 of 22 districts. Monaragala, Puttalam and the Vanni have no reviews in either corpus and are drawn hatched — that means not measured, never no complaints.

Change over time. A complaint stays in the data long after the problem is fixed — a road repaired in 2022 is still described as broken by every review written before it. The small chart on each row shows the complaint rate in four periods — up to 2018, 2019–20, 2021–22 and 2023–24 — oldest on the left, and the size of each dot shows how many opinions that point rests on. A dramatic-looking swing built on four reviews therefore looks like four reviews. Click any aspect for the same figures as a table.

We also tested whether old reviews should simply be given less weight, and concluded they should not. The correlation between a destination’s historical complaint rate and its current one is about 0.6 and flat from one year out to seven — a 2017 rate predicts the 2024 rate as well as a 2023 rate does. Age alone does not make a review less informative here, so down-weighting it would discard valid evidence. No decay is applied and no verdict is issued; the rates are shown and the reader judges.

Method. Reviews are split into single-opinion segments, tagged by aspect, then classified as complaint / praise / factual by a three-class transformer with a documented domain correction. Aspect and polarity labels are generated automatically and carry error; accuracy is measured against a human-annotated gold set of 200 segments covering roads, cleanliness, facilities and safety. Price, crowding and scenery are reported descriptively and are outside that measured scope.
